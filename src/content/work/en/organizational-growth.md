## Reframing the problem

**If we only ask:** How can more projects be delivered successfully?

**The more useful question:** Which decisions depend on individual experience, and which steps can become methods someone else can use? Examine how study design, records, and handoffs connect.

## A recurring team problem

When every project starts from scratch, experienced staff become a dependency at every step. A new request means understanding the need while finding methods, preparing equipment, and organizing delivery again.

Since 2019, I have been responsible for building consulting and solution-delivery capability: direction, proposals, team development, key clients, and complex projects.

## What I put in place

I made goals, constraints, and pending decisions explicit in discovery. We documented reusable research steps and records, with clear responsibilities for client communication, design, equipment, fieldwork, analysis, and reporting.

Reviews kept more than the final report: why a design was chosen, where it failed, and what another project could reuse. Those records fed into proposals, training, and knowledge retrieval.

## What it produced

The team developed service areas in consumer research, user experience, and medical-device usability, alongside work in automotive, architecture, and aviation. I contributed methods, team leadership, and business development; the results are collaborative.

This account describes how the practice developed. There are no public commercial measures or efficiency comparisons here to establish a growth rate. The practical test is whether the next project can use and improve the materials.
